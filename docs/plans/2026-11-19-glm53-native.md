# Plan: GLM-5.3 full uncensored on ds4-native (`GlmMoeDsa` port) — 2026-11-19

Status: **in progress**: Phase 2 (weights) running, Phase 3 step 1 (Semantics oracle) done 2026-11-19. Companion to
[`docs/research/2026-10-26-feasibility.md`](../research/2026-10-26-feasibility.md), whose measured
constants (miss cost, H2D, GEMV, locality table) this plan reuses. Everything GLM-specific is
marked **est.** and must be replaced by measurement before it drives a design decision.

## Settled decisions (this plan)

1. **Target checkpoint:** `kitaniai/GLM-5.3-UNCENSORED-MXFP4` — v2 CRACK surgery
   (source rev `12170112`, published 2026-08-30, the 0-loops release), MXFP4 routed experts +
   FP8/BF16 backbone, 394.65 GiB, all 256 experts / 75 MoE layers, full density. **Not** a 2-bit
   or pruned artifact.
2. **VARIANT flag: `glm-uncensored` | `glm-cyber`.** Both dealignai variants leave routed FP8
   experts unchanged from `JANGQ-AI/GLM-5.3-FP8`, so the expert MXFP4 tensors — and therefore the
   whole `experts.ds4n` store — are **identical for both**. Only the backbone differs
   (119 bf16 residual-writer edits; attn strength 4.0 layers 12–77, FFN 5.0 layers 20–77). One
   store, two backbones: the WARMIND pattern. Uncensored backbone comes from kitaniai itself;
   cyber backbone fetches non-expert tensors from `dealignai/GLM-5.3-CYBERSECURITY-FP8`.
3. **Speed expectation (est.):** 15–25 tok/s exact, 30–50 tok/s with cache-conditional routing +
   ~70 GB RAM victim tier. This is a **quality/architecture play**, not a speed play; the
   all-resident floor (~150 tok/s at 23 GB/token over ~3.6 TB/s) is unreachable while streaming.
4. **Quality expectation:** CRACK v2 numbers carry (MMLU 87.43 +1.85 pp, 91.7% TRUE_COMPLY harm
   surface) because the surgery lives in backbone tensors kitaniai kept at FP8/BF16; the only
   added loss is the FP8→MXFP4 expert requant — the same precision class DS4.1 ships natively.
5. **Cyber benchmarks:** no local artifact in this family has a published task-level cyber eval.
   The only claims: zai base (CyberGym 84.5, ExploitBench 54.4) and a hosted-only
   abliteration.ai model (84.5, unaudited, not runnable locally). CyberGym eval is scheduled
   (Phase 5 verdict).
6. **No hardware purchase** (operator, 2026-11-19): Phases 0 and 1 as originally drafted are
   dropped — the Q2_K probe is skipped in favor of evaluating the real weights, and the 4 TB
   M.2_2 drive is deferred. Everything lives on `rpool` (444G free at plan revision), fitting the
   ~415G steady state with the incremental convert-and-delete flow in Phase 2. The M.2_2 drive
   remains the recorded lever for miss parallelism (est. 1.5–1.8×) if Path B earns a Phase 5
   keep.
7. **kitaniai carries the MTP block** (verified in `model.safetensors.index.json`: layer 78,
   1,562 tensors incl. `eh_proj`/`enorm`/`hnorm` and its own 256 experts) — no separate MTP
   fetch. Expert tensors are per-expert sextuplets (`experts.E.{gate,up,down}_proj.weight` +
   `weight_scale`), so one expert = one store record source, DeepSeek-style.

## Phase 2 — weights pipeline (start: 2026-11-19, download running)

- ~~Phase 0 probe and Phase 1 hardware~~ — dropped per settled decision 6.
- Fetch kitaniai to `/models/glm53-native/kitaniai` on `rpool` (`hf download`, resumable,
  per-file sha; est. 5.7 h at ~20 MB/s). Whole-repo download fits the ~444G free with ~49G
  margin; `nix-collect-garbage` runs alongside for extra headroom. Stopping on ENOSPC is safe
  (resume), monitored.
- Convert routed experts → `experts.ds4n` (GLM) store: 19,200 records ≈ 20.03 MB, target layout
  identical to DS4.1's; verify every record. **Incremental convert-and-delete:** the converter
  walks layer-40-style expert sextuplets from the 11 MB index, appends verified records to the
  store, then deletes the consumed source tensors/shards — source shrinks 394.7G → metadata-only
  while the store grows 0 → 384.6G, so peak usage never exceeds the download peak. Backbone A
  (uncensored) from kitaniai's non-expert tensors, est. 26–30 GB.
- **Correction (2026-11-19, from the shard index):** that peak claim holds only if each shard is
  deleted as soon as everything in it is saved. Kitaniai has 282 shards: **197 are expert-only
  (261.2 GiB)**, but **85 interleave backbone tensors with Experts (133.4 GiB)**, mostly attention
  next to the layer's first Experts. Converting every Expert first and then deleting the
  expert-only shards ends ~90 GiB above the download peak, and rpool has ~8 GiB left when the
  download completes. The order that fits is **per shard, in layer order**: convert and verify
  the layers its Experts belong to, extract its backbone tensors into Backbone A (one part file
  per source shard), and only then delete it. That keeps the peak at the download peak plus about
  one layer (5 GiB). `ds4n-glm53-store-build` now lists only shards whose every tensor is a
  *verified* stored-Expert tensor, so expert-only shards go first, and it refuses to write
  without `--min-free-gib` (default 8) headroom.
- **Backbone extractor (built 2026-11-19, branch `glm53/engine-core`):**
  `ds4n-glm53-backbone-extract` copies each shard's Backbone tensors into a **Backbone part**
  (`/models/glm53-native/backbone-uncensored/backbone-NNNNN-of-00282.safetensors`, plain
  safetensors, byte for byte). Writes are atomic and retry-safe, and `--verify` proves every part
  equals its shard's plan. The Backbone is defined as the complement of the store
  (`core/glm53/backbone` `is_backbone`), so every tensor lands in exactly one place, and the MTP
  block's 256 Experts go into the parts. `ds4n-glm53-store-build --verify --backbone DIR` lists a
  shard as deletable only when its Experts' records **and** its part both verify in that run.
  Checked on real shards 2–5 (RAM tmpfs): 24 Backbone tensors verified bit-identical by the
  official safetensors reader, shard 2 (mixed) released, shard 5 kept (layer 11 not converted),
  and one flipped byte in a part kept its shard.
- **Steady state is exactly the download.** The store's records hold the same 17 bytes per 32
  weights as kitaniai's Expert tensors: 358.6 GiB of data, with the zero padding compressed away
  (only in the `rpool/models/glm53-store` dataset, `compression=zle`: `/models` itself is `compression=off`, where the padding would cost ~16.4 GiB). The Backbone parts are the rest (~36 GiB, MTP Experts included). Consumed shard by
  shard, the pool ends where the download left it (~8 GiB free), and the peak is that plus one
  range in flight (1 layer ≈ 4.8 GiB). **The consume must therefore run with a floor below
  the default 8 GiB** (the driver uses 2): there is no other way to begin. Free space means the
  dataset's `avail` (`zfs list`, `df`), never `zpool list` FREE: that includes the slop reserve
  (~57 GiB here) no dataset can use, and a conversion sized by it filled every dataset on the
  pool on 2026-11-19. **Backbone B (cyber) does not fit on rpool** next to that. It needs its own
  space (est. 20–30 GiB) freed first, or the M.2_2 lever.
- **Release is per tensor (correction, 2026-11-19, before any shard was consumed).** 68 of the
  282 shards hold Experts of two layers, and 181 Experts' sextuplets span two shards. A rule
  of "a layer verified whole in the same run" can never release such a shard: by the next
  layer's pass, the earlier layer's other shards are gone, so that layer is never whole on disk
  again (~100 GiB stranded). `decode_record` inverts `build_record` (and refuses non-zero
  padding), so each stored tensor is proven on its own against its slice of its record.
  `tools/glm53-store/consume.sh` drives the whole consume with that rule, walking the shards in
  **file order** (kitaniai orders shards by the string order of layer numbers, so a numeric layer
  order would hold up to 8.9 GiB of duplicate bytes) and converting per Expert as soon as its six
  tensors are on disk. Its window is 1–2 shards, so the peak is about one shard over the steady
  state.
- Backbone B (cyber): fetch non-expert tensors from dealignai CYBERSECURITY-FP8; `DERIVATIVE.json`
  + `tools/hf-backbone` verify against the FP8 source, exactly as WARMIND does.
- Receipts: kitaniai ships `conversion.json` + `artifact_manifest.json`; keep `CRACK_SURGERY.json`
  per variant. ~~Steady disk: ~445 GB (store 385 + 2 backbones 60) split across both pools; the
  kitaniai source stays as the archive for re-conversion/QA.~~ There is one pool (decision 6), and
  the source cannot stay: it is consumed shard by shard (see the correction above). Re-conversion
  means re-downloading.

## Phase 3 — engine port (2–4 weeks, Loom-governed, in a worktree)

- **Executor:** 78 layers, MLA (kv_lora 512, rope 64, v_head 256), DSA lightning indexer
  (32 heads × 128, top-k 2048, full/shared every-4 pattern, `index_skip_topk_offset` 3, MTP
  iteration share), sigmoid router top-8 of 256 + 1 shared, `routed_scaling_factor` 2.5, first 3
  dense layers. Functional core stays pure; I/O at the shell; fallible paths return `Either`.
- **Kernels:** reuse the MXFP4 block-scaled GEMV; new shapes 6144×2048×3. First Exactness target:
  GEMV vs CPU oracle ≥ 99.97% bit-identical (the DS4.1 bar). Verify item: Quark's E8M0 scale
  layout/grouping matches the kernel's per-32 assumption.
- **t_base rebuild (est. 8–11 ms):** ~162 TP syncs/token over PCIe x8 (no NVLink) — measure first,
  then CCR and the RAM tier as speed work.
- **MTP (1 block):** port last; fragile even on stock vLLM per dealignai's field notes. Stretch
  goal, not a dependency.
- **Exactness harness:** reuse `ds4n-serve-check` (Sessions, rewind, Shared Rounds invariance).
- Production conflict per the feasibility risk list: every GPU experiment is flare/warmind
  downtime — development is CPU/oracle-first; GPU windows are scheduled.

### Phase 3 progress

**Step 1 — Semantics oracle (done 2026-11-19, branch `glm53/engine-core`).** The new engine
lives in `src/core/glm53/` beside DS4.1's, never inside it (the existing engine is untouched; it
serves ds41-flash).

- *Reference pinned* (the plan's one real design decision): **transformers 5.16.0**
  `modeling_glm_moe_dsa.py`, vendored with the released `config.json` in `ref/glm-5.3/`
  (`README.md` there gives the decision and the easy-to-miss details). It is the version kitaniai
  converted with, it is the definitional implementation, and it runs on CPU. vLLM, llama.cpp and
  B12X stay as later cross-checks of throughput numerics.
- `core/glm53/config`: `parse_config` reads a config.json, rejects every architecture the engine
  does not implement by name, and gives each layer its `IndexerKind` (Full/Shared) and `MlpKind`.
  The released file parses to exactly the Expert store's dimensions (tested).
- `core/glm53/reference`: the FP32 **Semantics oracle**. It covers absorbed MLA over **Latent
  rows**, the DSA indexer with Full/Shared **Selections**, sigmoid `noaux_tc` routing, the
  shared expert and the dense layers, decoded token by token from its own caches. `bind_model`
  binds HF names and shapes, per-Expert or fused.
- Golden anchor: `tools/glm53-golden/make_golden.py` runs a tiny random `GlmMoeDsa` (all
  structural features, top-k 5 of 16 positions) on CPU in the dev image, choosing a seed whose
  every top-k has margin > 1e-3. Decoding token by token reproduces the reference's causal
  prefill at **1.6e-6** relative (hidden), 1.5e-6 (logits) and 3.5e-6 (Latent rows), with every
  Selection identical. `tools/glm53-golden/mutation-probe.sh` kills 10/10 meaningful mutations;
  one equivalent mutant is documented.

**Next steps, in order:**

1. *Real-weights loader (shell):* a `TensorLookup` over the Backbone parts (plain safetensors,
   original tensor names, so one header scan per part builds the lookup) that
   dequantizes FP8/BF16 → FP32, plus an Expert source that dequantizes store records. This
   replaces `MoeWeights::experts` (a `std::vector<Mlp>` today, the only adapter so far) with a
   seam, which is deepen work once the second adapter exists. Then run the Semantics oracle for a
   few tokens of a real prompt at real dimensions: the first real-weights check, CPU only.
2. *Reference configuration design:* decide the activation precision into MXFP4 linears (see Open
   items) and the reduction orders. Write the bit-exact oracle on top of the semantics one, as
   `layer_reference` did for DS4.1.
3. *Kernels* (first GPU window): MXFP4 GEMV at 6144×2048 (routed **and shared** Experts), MLA
   decode over Latent rows, the indexer and top-2048.

## Open items

- ~~Confirm kitaniai carries the MTP tensors~~ — verified, settled decision 7 (layer 78 present,
  1,562 tensors incl. `eh_proj`/`enorm`/`hnorm` and its own 256 experts).
- Watch llama.cpp #31281 and PR #27917 (Flash MTP) — upstream fixes simplify future GGUF-based
  cross-checks and the MTP port.
- ~~WiFi download reliability: use resumable fetch with per-shard sha256~~ — `hf download`
  (resume + etag verification) runs the fetch; monitored.
- ~~Confirm GLM-5.3 has no sliding-window attention~~: confirmed by the pinned reference. Every
  layer is `deepseek_sparse_attention` (DSA over all positions), with no window.
- **Activation precision into MXFP4 linears.** kitaniai's `quantization_config` declares
  `input_tensors: fp4, dynamic, per_group 32, e8m0` (W4A4) for every quantized linear: the routed
  Experts **and the shared Experts**. Everything else is excluded and stays FP8/BF16 (all
  attention, routers, indexers, the dense layers 0–2, and the MTP block's `eh_proj`). `vLLM`'s
  Quark path emulates MXFP4 activations. DS4.1's kernels feed E4M3 activations. Decide which
  the Reference configuration uses by measuring both against the Semantics oracle (KL over a
  prompt set). `inference_tested: false` in kitaniai's `conversion.json` means nobody has
  measured it yet.
- **RoPE angles at long context.** The reference rounds `pos * inv_freq` to FP32, about
  `pos * 2^-24` rad of error for the fastest pair (~0.06 rad at 2^20). The executor must round the
  same way to match, or deviate on purpose and say so.
- **Selection ties.** ReLU makes exact-zero index scores common (most golden seeds had a 0
  margin at the k-th boundary), so ties at the 2048th position will be frequent on real prompts.
  torch's order is unspecified, and the oracle's ties go to the lower position. Measure the tie
  rate on real weights before the executor's top-k kernel is designed.

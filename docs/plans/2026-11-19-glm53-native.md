# Plan: GLM-5.3 full uncensored on ds4-native (`GlmMoeDsa` port) — 2026-11-19

Status: **in progress**: Phase 2 (weights) done 2026-11-19 (store + Backbone A on `rpool/models/glm53-store`);
Phase 3 steps 1–4 (Semantics oracle, first real-weights run, activation precision measured, the
MoE's Reference configuration) done 2026-11-19. Companion to
[`docs/research/2026-10-26-feasibility.md`](../research/2026-10-26-feasibility.md), whose measured
constants (miss cost, H2D, GEMV, locality table) this plan reuses. Everything GLM-specific is
marked **est.** and must be replaced by measurement before it drives a design decision.

## Settled decisions (this plan)

1. **Target checkpoint:** `kitaniai/GLM-5.3-UNCENSORED-MXFP4` — v2 CRACK surgery
   (source rev `12170112`, published 2026-08-30, the 0-loops release), MXFP4 routed experts +
   FP8/BF16 backbone, 394.65 GiB, all 256 experts / 75 MoE layers, full density. **Not** a 2-bit
   or pruned artifact.
2. **VARIANT flag: `glm-uncensored` | `glm-cyber`.** Both dealignai variants leave routed FP8
   experts unchanged from `JANGQ-AI/GLM-5.3-FP8`, so the expert MXFP4 tensors — and therefore the
   whole `experts.ds4n` store — are **identical for both**. Only the backbone differs
   (119 bf16 residual-writer edits; attn strength 4.0 layers 12–77, FFN 5.0 layers 20–77). One
   store, two backbones: the WARMIND pattern. Uncensored backbone comes from kitaniai itself;
   cyber backbone fetches non-expert tensors from `dealignai/GLM-5.3-CYBERSECURITY-FP8`.
3. **Speed expectation (est.):** 15–25 tok/s exact, 30–50 tok/s with cache-conditional routing +
   ~70 GB RAM victim tier. This is a **quality/architecture play**, not a speed play; the
   all-resident floor (~150 tok/s at 23 GB/token over ~3.6 TB/s) is unreachable while streaming.
4. **Quality expectation:** CRACK v2 numbers carry (MMLU 87.43 +1.85 pp, 91.7% TRUE_COMPLY harm
   surface), est., not yet measured on kitaniai's requant (`inference_tested: false`). The
   surgery's attention half (`o_proj`, layers 12–77) is BF16 in kitaniai. Its FFN half (layers
   20–77) can only be on the shared Experts' `down_proj`, since the routed Experts are unchanged,
   and kitaniai requantized the shared Experts to MXFP4 (Phase 3 step 2: the Backbone has no FP8).
   So the added loss is the FP8→MXFP4 requant of the routed Experts and of half the surgery. The
   E4M3 activation decision (8) keeps that edited projection's input closest to FP32.
5. **Cyber benchmarks:** no local artifact in this family has a published task-level cyber eval.
   The only claims: zai base (CyberGym 84.5, ExploitBench 54.4) and a hosted-only
   abliteration.ai model (84.5, unaudited, not runnable locally). CyberGym eval is scheduled
   (Phase 5 verdict).
6. **No hardware purchase** (operator, 2026-11-19): Phases 0 and 1 as originally drafted are
   dropped — the Q2_K probe is skipped in favor of evaluating the real weights, and the 4 TB
   M.2_2 drive is deferred. Everything lives on `rpool` (444G free at plan revision), fitting the
   ~415G steady state with the incremental convert-and-delete flow in Phase 2. The M.2_2 drive
   remains the recorded lever for miss parallelism (est. 1.5–1.8×) if Path B earns a Phase 5
   keep.
7. **kitaniai carries the MTP block** (verified in `model.safetensors.index.json`: layer 78,
   1,562 tensors incl. `eh_proj`/`enorm`/`hnorm` and its own 256 experts) — no separate MTP
   fetch. Expert tensors are per-expert sextuplets (`experts.E.{gate,up,down}_proj.weight` +
   `weight_scale`), so one expert = one store record source, DeepSeek-style.
8. **Experts' activation precision: E4M3 (W4A8), not the declared W4A4** (operator,
   2026-11-19, Phase 3 step 3). It applies to routed and shared Experts. Mean KL against the FP32
   oracle is 0.0051 nats, against 0.0616 for W4A4 (BF16: 0.0036). It is an engine choice and
   leaves the checkpoint, the store and both VARIANT backbones untouched. This is the Reference
   configuration's first fixed choice.

## Phase 2 — weights pipeline (start: 2026-11-19, download running)

- ~~Phase 0 probe and Phase 1 hardware~~ — dropped per settled decision 6.
- Fetch kitaniai to `/models/glm53-native/kitaniai` on `rpool` (`hf download`, resumable,
  per-file sha; est. 5.7 h at ~20 MB/s). Whole-repo download fits the ~444G free with ~49G
  margin; `nix-collect-garbage` runs alongside for extra headroom. Stopping on ENOSPC is safe
  (resume), monitored.
- Convert routed experts → `experts.ds4n` (GLM) store: 19,200 records ≈ 20.03 MB, target layout
  identical to DS4.1's; verify every record. **Incremental convert-and-delete:** the converter
  walks layer-40-style expert sextuplets from the 11 MB index, appends verified records to the
  store, then deletes the consumed source tensors/shards — source shrinks 394.7G → metadata-only
  while the store grows 0 → 384.6G, so peak usage never exceeds the download peak. Backbone A
  (uncensored) from kitaniai's non-expert tensors, est. 26–30 GB.
- **Correction (2026-11-19, from the shard index):** that peak claim holds only if each shard is
  deleted as soon as everything in it is saved. Kitaniai has 282 shards: **197 are expert-only
  (261.2 GiB)**, but **85 interleave backbone tensors with Experts (133.4 GiB)**, mostly attention
  next to the layer's first Experts. Converting every Expert first and then deleting the
  expert-only shards ends ~90 GiB above the download peak, and rpool has ~8 GiB left when the
  download completes. The order that fits is **per shard, in layer order**: convert and verify
  the layers its Experts belong to, extract its backbone tensors into Backbone A (one part file
  per source shard), and only then delete it. That keeps the peak at the download peak plus about
  one layer (5 GiB). `ds4n-glm53-store-build` now lists only shards whose every tensor is a
  *verified* stored-Expert tensor, so expert-only shards go first, and it refuses to write
  without `--min-free-gib` (default 8) headroom.
- **Backbone extractor (built 2026-11-19, branch `glm53/engine-core`):**
  `ds4n-glm53-backbone-extract` copies each shard's Backbone tensors into a **Backbone part**
  (`/models/glm53-native/backbone-uncensored/backbone-NNNNN-of-00282.safetensors`, plain
  safetensors, byte for byte). Writes are atomic and retry-safe, and `--verify` proves every part
  equals its shard's plan. The Backbone is defined as the complement of the store
  (`core/glm53/backbone` `is_backbone`), so every tensor lands in exactly one place, and the MTP
  block's 256 Experts go into the parts. `ds4n-glm53-store-build --verify --backbone DIR` lists a
  shard as deletable only when its Experts' records **and** its part both verify in that run.
  Checked on real shards 2–5 (RAM tmpfs): 24 Backbone tensors verified bit-identical by the
  official safetensors reader, shard 2 (mixed) released, shard 5 kept (layer 11 not converted),
  and one flipped byte in a part kept its shard.
- **Steady state is exactly the download.** The store's records hold the same 17 bytes per 32
  weights as kitaniai's Expert tensors: 358.6 GiB of data, with the zero padding compressed away
  (only in the `rpool/models/glm53-store` dataset, `compression=zle`: `/models` itself is `compression=off`, where the padding would cost ~16.4 GiB). The Backbone parts are the rest (~36 GiB, MTP Experts included). Consumed shard by
  shard, the pool ends where the download left it (~8 GiB free), and the peak is that plus one
  range in flight (1 layer ≈ 4.8 GiB). **The consume must therefore run with a floor below
  the default 8 GiB** (the driver uses 2): there is no other way to begin. Free space means the
  dataset's `avail` (`zfs list`, `df`), never `zpool list` FREE: that includes the slop reserve
  (~57 GiB here) no dataset can use, and a conversion sized by it filled every dataset on the
  pool on 2026-11-19. **Backbone B (cyber):** 2026-11-20, 32 GiB freed (ComfyUI Krea2 family,
  receipt `/models/comfyui/archive-receipt-krea2-2026-11-20.txt`), so rpool has 39.1 GiB free. Stored
  as a delta over Backbone A (≈ 14 GB, est.: 66 `o_proj` + 58 shared `down_proj`), it fits; a
  full 37 GiB copy does not (`docs/research/2026-11-20-glm53-speed-tradeoffs.md`, "Disk").
- **Release is per tensor (correction, 2026-11-19, before any shard was consumed).** 68 of the
  282 shards hold Experts of two layers, and 181 Experts' sextuplets span two shards. A rule
  of "a layer verified whole in the same run" can never release such a shard: by the next
  layer's pass, the earlier layer's other shards are gone, so that layer is never whole on disk
  again (~100 GiB stranded). `decode_record` inverts `build_record` (and refuses non-zero
  padding), so each stored tensor is proven on its own against its slice of its record.
  `tools/glm53-store/consume.sh` drives the whole consume with that rule, walking the shards in
  **file order** (kitaniai orders shards by the string order of layer numbers, so a numeric layer
  order would hold up to 8.9 GiB of duplicate bytes) and converting per Expert as soon as its six
  tensors are on disk. Its window is 1–2 shards, so the peak is about one shard over the steady
  state.
- Backbone B (cyber): fetch non-expert tensors from dealignai CYBERSECURITY-FP8; `DERIVATIVE.json`
  + `tools/hf-backbone` verify against the FP8 source, exactly as WARMIND does.
- Receipts: kitaniai ships `conversion.json` + `artifact_manifest.json`; keep `CRACK_SURGERY.json`
  per variant. ~~Steady disk: ~445 GB (store 385 + 2 backbones 60) split across both pools; the
  kitaniai source stays as the archive for re-conversion/QA.~~ There is one pool (decision 6), and
  the source cannot stay: it is consumed shard by shard (see the correction above). Re-conversion
  means re-downloading.

## Phase 3 — engine port (2–4 weeks, Loom-governed, in a worktree)

- **Executor:** 78 layers, MLA (kv_lora 512, rope 64, v_head 256), DSA lightning indexer
  (32 heads × 128, top-k 2048, full/shared every-4 pattern, `index_skip_topk_offset` 3, MTP
  iteration share), sigmoid router top-8 of 256 + 1 shared, `routed_scaling_factor` 2.5, first 3
  dense layers. Functional core stays pure; I/O at the shell; fallible paths return `Either`.
- **Kernels:** reuse the MXFP4 block-scaled GEMV; new shapes 6144×2048×3. First Exactness target:
  GEMV vs CPU oracle ≥ 99.97% bit-identical (the DS4.1 bar). Verify item: Quark's E8M0 scale
  layout/grouping matches the kernel's per-32 assumption.
- **t_base rebuild (est. 8–11 ms):** ~162 TP syncs/token over PCIe x8 (no NVLink) — measure first,
  then CCR and the RAM tier as speed work.
- **MTP (1 block):** port last; fragile even on stock vLLM per dealignai's field notes. Stretch
  goal, not a dependency.
- **Exactness harness:** reuse `ds4n-serve-check` (Sessions, rewind, Shared Rounds invariance).
- Production conflict per the feasibility risk list: every GPU experiment is flare/warmind
  downtime — development is CPU/oracle-first; GPU windows are scheduled.

### Phase 3 progress

**Step 1 — Semantics oracle (done 2026-11-19, branch `glm53/engine-core`).** The new engine
lives in `src/core/glm53/` beside DS4.1's, never inside it (the existing engine is untouched; it
serves ds41-flash).

- *Reference pinned* (the plan's one real design decision): **transformers 5.16.0**
  `modeling_glm_moe_dsa.py`, vendored with the released `config.json` in `ref/glm-5.3/`
  (`README.md` there gives the decision and the easy-to-miss details). It is the version kitaniai
  converted with, it is the definitional implementation, and it runs on CPU. vLLM, llama.cpp and
  B12X stay as later cross-checks of throughput numerics.
- `core/glm53/config`: `parse_config` reads a config.json, rejects every architecture the engine
  does not implement by name, and gives each layer its `IndexerKind` (Full/Shared) and `MlpKind`.
  The released file parses to exactly the Expert store's dimensions (tested).
- `core/glm53/reference`: the FP32 **Semantics oracle**. It covers absorbed MLA over **Latent
  rows**, the DSA indexer with Full/Shared **Selections**, sigmoid `noaux_tc` routing, the
  shared expert and the dense layers, decoded token by token from its own caches. `bind_model`
  binds HF names and shapes, per-Expert or fused.
- Golden anchor: `tools/glm53-golden/make_golden.py` runs a tiny random `GlmMoeDsa` (all
  structural features, top-k 5 of 16 positions) on CPU in the dev image, choosing a seed whose
  every top-k has margin > 1e-3. Decoding token by token reproduces the reference's causal
  prefill at **1.6e-6** relative (hidden), 1.5e-6 (logits) and 3.5e-6 (Latent rows), with every
  Selection identical. `tools/glm53-golden/mutation-probe.sh` kills 12/12 meaningful mutations
  (including a routed Expert's output combined out of Route order, and the wrong Expert run);
  one equivalent mutant is documented.

**Step 2 — first real-weights run (done 2026-11-19, CPU, beside the live flare deployment).**
The model does not fit in RAM as FP32 (~66 GB of Backbone, ~1.4 TB of routed Experts), so the
oracle's seam moved (deepen):

- `MoeWeights` is now only a Sparse layer's Backbone (router, bias, shared expert). Decoding a
  layer splits at the routed Experts: `begin_layer` (attention, the residual, routing: its
  `Route` names the Experts it needs) and `finish_layer` (given exactly those Experts; since
  step 3, their outputs).
  `bind_layer` binds one layer's Backbone. `decode_layer`/`decode_token` compose the same pieces
  over `bind_model`'s fully bound tiny model, and a test proves layer-major decoding with
  per-layer binding is bit-identical to them on the golden.
- `core/glm53/dequant`: BF16/F32 widening and checkpoint MXFP4 → FP32, exact (checked element by
  element against the store's GGML conversion), and a whole Expert record → FP32.
  **The Backbone holds no FP8**: it is BF16, the routers' F32 bias, and MXFP4 shared Experts.
- `io/glm53_weights` `BackboneParts` (one header scan over the 88 parts; a layer loads on
  threads) and `io/glm53_store` `StoreReader` (refuses an unwritten record: an all-zero record
  decodes to an Expert of zeros).
- `ds4n-glm53-oracle-run` runs layer-major: one layer bound, every token of the batch through
  `begin_layer` in position order, the union of their routed Experts read in groups, then the
  tokens finished in parallel. Each generated token is a batch of one.

Result (receipts `docs/bench/glm53-oracle/2026-11-19-capitals.*`; 21-token prompt "The capital
of France is Paris. The capital of Germany is Berlin. The capital of Italy is", 3 greedy tokens,
478 s on 16 threads, flare unaffected):

- **The semantics hold on the real weights.** " Rome" at p = 0.994; " Paris" (0.77) and
  " Berlin" (0.98) top-1 where the prompt says them; the third "The capital of" ranks " Italy"
  first, a country not used yet; greedy continuation " Rome. The".
- **Route margins** (the worst chosen biased score minus the best unchosen, 1,725 routes): median
  3.5e-3, 10th percentile 2.9e-4, minimum 1.9e-6; 67 below 1e-4 and 9 below 1e-5. The median is
  about one BF16 step (2^-8 ≈ 3.9e-3), so the router logits must stay FP32, and the routes
  within ~1e-5 depend on the reduction order: the Reference configuration must fix it.
- **Activation precision** at every feed-forward input (gate/up of every routed and shared
  Expert): MXFP4 (W4A4) round trip 11.6% relative error (mean, max 18.0%), E4M3 2.65% (max 3.7%).
  That is the size of the input noise. The decision still needs its effect on the logits (KL):
  step 3. (This MXFP4 figure used the OCP floor scale; step 3 uses the checkpoint's own Quark
  "even" scale.)
- **Selections**: 483 on Full layers, none dropping a position (a prompt under 2048 tokens keeps
  all). The Selection tie rate needs a prompt over 2048 tokens: on CPU that is hours of attention,
  so it moves to the bit-exact oracle or the first GPU window.

**Step 3 — Activation precision measured (done 2026-11-19, CPU, flare stopped for the run with
operator approval; left stopped afterwards).** The question: what the Experts' linears should read
their inputs in. The checkpoint declares W4A4: MXFP4 inputs to every routed and shared Expert
linear. DS4.1's kernels use E4M3.

- `core/glm53/precision`: `ActivationPrecision` {FP32, BF16, E4M3, MXFP4}, one round trip
  each. MXFP4 follows kitaniai's `quantization_config` exactly: AMD Quark, `scale_calculation_mode:
  even`. Read from the amd-quark 0.13 source (`even_round`, and the Triton kernel's
  `(amax + 0x200000) & 0x7F800000`), the shared exponent rounds up once amax's mantissa reaches
  1.75. Elements round half to even. Step 2's OCP floor would saturate those blocks instead.
- The oracle's seam moved again (deepen): `expert_outputs` runs one Expert over every token it
  serves (the Grouped MoE shape; batched linears give each output exactly the bits it gets alone)
  at a precision, rounding both linear inputs (the feed-forward input, and silu(gate) × up before
  down_proj). `finish_layer` takes the Route's `ExpertOutput`s, and its shared expert runs at
  their precision, which must agree. A Dense layer's MLP stays FP32 (excluded from W4A4). A block
  format on widths that are not whole 32-blocks is an error, never a zeroed tail. The
  layer-major test now runs Expert-major and is still bit-identical to `decode_token`.
- `ds4n-glm53-oracle-run --prompt … --prompt … --activations fp32,bf16,e4m3,mxfp4`: every
  (prompt, precision) is a stream. Each layer reads the union of its Experts once for all streams
  (≈250 per layer instead of a re-read per token group), and every precision is compared with the
  first by KL over the full vocabulary at every prompt position.

Result (receipts `docs/bench/glm53-oracle/2026-11-19-activations.*`, prompts in its
`.prompts.md`): five prompts, 315 positions: the capitals prompt, English prose, Python, worked
arithmetic and Chinese. 20 streams, 1,961 s on 30 threads. Prompt 0 at FP32 reproduces step 2's
21 lines exactly, so neither the new schedule nor the batched linears changed any bits.

| Experts' activations | KL vs FP32, mean | median | p90 | max | top-1 agrees | perplexity | Routes ≠ FP32's |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FP32 (oracle) | — | — | — | — | — | 3.849 | — |
| BF16 (W4A16) | 0.0036 | 0.0002 | 0.0066 | 0.198 | 314/315 | 3.826 | 30% |
| E4M3 (W4A8) | 0.0051 | 0.0011 | 0.0140 | 0.107 | 308/315 | 3.805 | 47% |
| MXFP4 (W4A4, declared) | **0.0616** | 0.0120 | 0.1354 | 2.43 | 287/315 | **3.975** | 73% |

- **MXFP4 loses about 12× what E4M3 does.** It costs 3.3% perplexity and 9% of top-1 picks, and
  it is worst on every prompt, especially arithmetic (prompt 3: mean KL 0.116, one position 2.4
  nats). E4M3 is within ~1.4× of BF16, and both are below the perplexity noise between the
  prompts.
- **Routes flip freely and harmlessly**: 30% of routes differ from FP32's already at BF16, while
  KL stays ~0.004. Most real routes are near ties (step 2), so a different precision picks a
  different but near-equivalent Expert. Route identity across engines is therefore no quality
  measure. Only one fixed arithmetic (the Reference configuration) can make routes reproducible.
- **Recommendation: E4M3 activations (W4A8) for routed and shared Experts, not the declared
  W4A4.** Quality: ≈ BF16. Speed: single-stream decode reads MXFP4 weights either way (memory
  bound, so the activation format costs no bandwidth). Prefill on SM120 runs FP4×FP8 MMA at the
  FP8 rate, about half of FP4×FP4 (est.). DS4.1's E4M3 activation path (`mx::act_quant`) is
  reused. **Decided** (operator, 2026-11-19): settled decision 8.

**Step 4 — the MoE's Reference configuration (done 2026-11-19, CPU).** Design:
[`docs/design/glm53-reference-configuration.md`](../design/glm53-reference-configuration.md),
which fixes every rounding point and summation order of the MoE and states, per operation,
what the attention side will reuse (fixed with its kernels).

- `core/glm53/exact_moe`: the bit-exact MoE oracle. It runs on the Expert **codes**
  (`Fp4Expert`, GGML blocks converted losslessly from the checkpoint's sextuplet), never their
  FP32 values, because the MMA aligns products by their operands' exponents. It is composed
  from DS4.1's hardware-proven definitions, used unchanged: `mx::act_quant`, `mx::fp4_row_dot`,
  `moe::half_hidden`/`half_down`/`combine`/`finalize`, `reduce::bf16_gemv`, `det::`. The router
  runs in 6 planes of 1024 with sigmoid in `det::` ops and ties to the lower id. Each Expert is
  2 Halves, with gate/up in 2 split-K planes, SwiGLU unclamped and the weight folded before
  down. The combine is routed Halves in ascending id, then the shared Half, then the Exchange.
- Pinned by its bits, not only by tolerances: a hash of 512 tokens' routes, weights, hidden
  fragments, partials and outputs (`kReferenceBits`). Independent witnesses back it: the design
  text written in double agrees within 1e-3, and the Semantics oracle at E4M3 within two E4M3
  roundings. `tools/glm53-golden/exact-moe-mutation-probe.sh`: 16/16 killed. The split-K planes
  turned out to be visible only when block scales differ widely, so the pinned layer spreads
  them (design, "How the bits are pinned").
- `io`: `StoreReader::read_tensors` (a record's codes, not its values) and
  `BackboneParts::load_shared_expert`. `finish_layer` now also takes a feed-forward computed
  elsewhere (`FeedForward`). `ds4n-glm53-oracle-run --activations exact` runs `exact_moe` for
  every Sparse layer inside the Semantics oracle, so its cost is measured as KL against FP32 like
  the other precisions.

Result on real weights (receipts `docs/bench/glm53-oracle/2026-11-19-exact-moe.*`, step 3's
five prompts, `--activations fp32,e4m3,exact`, 3,582 s on 30 threads):

| Experts | KL vs FP32, mean | median | p90 | max | top-1 agrees | perplexity | Routes ≠ FP32's |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E4M3 (Semantics oracle) | 0.0051 | 0.0011 | 0.0140 | 0.107 | 308/315 | 3.805 | 47% |
| `exact_moe` | **0.0091** | 0.0015 | 0.0217 | 0.409 | 306/315 | 3.816 | 50% |

- The FP32 and E4M3 streams reproduce step 3 line for line, so the new path changed nothing
  else.
- The Reference configuration's MoE costs ~1.8× E4M3 alone, higher on all five prompts and
  with no single bad site. Its extra BF16 roundings and the folded weight are the likely
  cause (BF16 alone: 0.0036). It is still far below W4A4 (0.0616), and perplexity stays under
  FP32's.
- Attributing the extra 0.004 nats: the rounding proxy (`--moe-roundings`, receipts
  `2026-11-19-moe-roundings.*`) rules out the BF16 roundings and the BF16 Exchange, so the
  Exchange stays BF16. The folded weight is worse only on the last layers' massive values
  (76–77) and better on 75; whether it costs KL needs an `exact` stream with the weight after
  down (design, "Open"). Until then the configuration stays DS4.1's kernels' form.

**Step 5 (first GPU window, the MoE): done 2026-11-19.**

- `ds4n-glm53-moe-check` gates the GPU MoE: **100% bit-identical** against `exact_moe` on real
  Experts. That covers the SwiGLU element function on 2^24 inputs, Rounds of 1–8 tokens, and
  sweeps of up to 300 tokens under each GPU Departure (layers 3, 40, 75, 77). The mutation probe
  kills 13/13.
- The pieces:
  - `core/glm53/moe_plan`: the Round's Plan, up to 64 jobs, more than DS4.1's 48;
  - `core/glm53/expert_tiles`: a Half in DS4.1's tiled layout;
  - `cuda/glm53/moe`: GLM's router, SwiGLU without clamp, and the combine;
  - `cuda/glm53/grouped_moe`: the Grouped MoE over a layer's tokens.
  DS4.1's kernels are reused unchanged, and DS4.1's sources are untouched.
- An 8-token Round runs at 173–342 µs per GPU, 1.3–1.5 TB/s.
- `ds4n-glm53-oracle-run --gpu N` runs the exact streams' Experts there, and `--activations
  exact-DEPARTURE` runs any `moe_roundings` Departure as a stream. The 4-stream KL run took
  1,713 s, against 3,582 s for 3 streams on the CPU, with `exact` identical at every position.
- **The folded weight costs no KL** (−5%, CI across zero): it stays.
- **The FP32 Exchange measured −31%** (0.0063 against 0.0091; CI [−0.0056, −0.0003]; lower on
  3 of 5 prompts). That is at the noise floor of re-rolled Routes, so it is not decided. A
  larger prompt set decides it (design, "Open"); until then the Exchange stays BF16.

**Next steps, in order:**

1. *Kernels* (first GPU window, continued): the FP32-vs-BF16 Exchange decision on a larger
   prompt set (cheap now with `--gpu`); MLA decode over Latent rows, the indexer and top-2048,
   whose exact orders are fixed with them (design, "By operation").
2. *The rest of the Reference configuration on CPU*, alongside: the Dense MLP, norms and
   attention projections in `reduce::bf16_gemv`/`hc::rmsnorm` form, so the whole-model exact
   decode has a CPU oracle before the GPU executor lands.

  prompt set). `inference_tested: false` in kitaniai's `conversion.json` means nobody had
  measured it. **Measured (Phase 3 step 3):** mean KL against FP32 is 0.0616 nats for W4A4 as
  declared (Quark even scale), 0.0051 for E4M3 and 0.0036 for BF16. **Decided: E4M3**
  (settled decision 8).
- **Route margins.** Real routes are close. Step 2: median 3.5e-3 between the 8th and 9th biased
  score, 9 of 1,725 within 1e-5. Step 3, five prompts: 82 of 23,625 within 1e-5, and 6 exact FP32
  ties (resolved to the lower id). Router logits must stay FP32, and the Reference configuration
  must fix the router's reduction order, or near-ties will flip between engines. They flip at
  little cost to quality (step 3: 30% of routes differ already at BF16 activations, mean KL
  0.004).
- **RoPE angles at long context.** The reference rounds `pos * inv_freq` to FP32, about
  `pos * 2^-24` rad of error for the fastest pair (~0.06 rad at 2^20). The executor must round the
  same way to match, or deviate on purpose and say so.
- **Selection ties.** ReLU makes exact-zero index scores common (most golden seeds had a 0
  margin at the k-th boundary), so ties at the 2048th position will be frequent on real prompts.
  torch's order is unspecified, and the oracle's ties go to the lower position. Measure the tie
  rate on real weights before the executor's top-k kernel is designed. Not measured yet: it needs
  a prompt over 2048 tokens, hours of CPU attention (Phase 3 step 2).

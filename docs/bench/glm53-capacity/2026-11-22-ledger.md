# GLM-5.3: the Ledger — Slots from byte arithmetic, not free memory (2026-11-22)

> **GPU gates, later the same day** (`docs/bench/glm53-counter/2026-11-22-counter-report.md`, §4):
> - The drift check passed on every open: each Claim was allocated byte for byte as planned.
> - The Slots came out one short everywhere: 98 at 4K, 88 at 64K, 82 with one 64K Cohort.
> - The cause: the reserve below was calibrated on nvidia-smi's card size (97,887 MiB), but `open` plans
>   on CUDA's, which is 101,973,491,712 B (97,249.5 MiB).
> - CUDA's total also moves by the MiB the driver reserves: GPU 0 read 3 MiB less by noon, and 64K had
>   5.7 MiB to spare. So the Ledger now plans each card in whole 256 MiB (`glm53::planned_card_bytes`;
>   an RTX PRO 6000 is planned at 97,024 MiB).
> - The reserve is **3.75 GiB**. The interval that reproduces the receipts is [3.390, 4.057] GiB, and
>   the GPUs open 99 / 90 / 83, confirmed.
> - The numbers below are the first calibration's, kept as written.

## Verdict

- **GLM-5.3's Expert Slots are now planned, not measured.**
  - `core/glm53/ledger` (pure, no I/O) restates every device allocation the engine makes, in six
    Claims per GPU: Exchange, Backbone, Cache, LayerScratch, Round and Sweep.
  - The Slots per Sparse layer are the card's bytes past those Claims and a runtime reserve, worked
    out with DS4.1's `ledger::plan_slots`.
  - The same settings on the same cards give the same Slots on every open, whatever happens to be
    free. Under cache-conditional routing (λ) that matters for more than speed: the Slots decide what
    is resident, so what is routed.
- **`Decoder::open` now plans first and loads second.**
  - Its first act is `plan_vram` over the cards' *total* VRAM. A context that cannot fit is refused
    in seconds, before 37 GB of Backbone is read.
  - It then counts what it actually asks `cudaMalloc` for, per Claim, and refuses to open on any
    difference ("the Ledger does not match the allocations …").
  - Free memory is read once, only as a safety check: the Slot pool plus 1 GiB of headroom must fit.
    Failing that names the likely cause, another process on the card or a reserve below what CUDA
    takes.
- **No Slot count changed by adopting it.**
  - The default runtime reserve, 4.75 GiB (`glm53::kDefaultRuntimeReserve`), reproduces both
    free-memory receipts: 99 Slots per layer at 4K and 83 at 64K, in the old layout where a sweep took
    the whole capacity.
  - Any reserve in (4.23, 4.90] GiB does. The fit shows CUDA itself took 1.3–2.0 GB beside the 3 GiB
    that free-memory sizing kept free.
- **`ds4n-glm53-ledger` answers "how many Slots at N context" without a GPU.** It touches no device, so
  it runs while WARMIND holds the cards.
- **Not yet run on a GPU.** The GPUs were serving WARMIND. Three gates are owed (below); the drift
  check is the one that matters.

## How the reserve was calibrated

Free-memory sizing gave `Slots = (free − 3 GiB) / (75 × 10,027,008 B)`, where
`free = card − claims − CUDA's own`. The Ledger's equivalent is
`Slots = (card − claims − reserve) / (75 × 10,027,008 B)`, so the reserve stands for 3 GiB plus
whatever CUDA took.

The card is 97,887 MiB = 102,641,958,912 B, and a Slot per layer is 752,025,600 B (0.70 GiB). GPU 0's
claims decide.

- **4K, old layout:**
  - claims are 22,894,180,184 B;
  - 99 Slots needs a reserve in (4,545,218,728; 5,297,244,328] B.
- **64K, old layout:**
  - claims are 34,962,378,584 B;
  - 83 Slots needs a reserve in (4,509,429,928; 5,261,455,528] B.
- **Both:** (4,545,218,728; 5,261,455,528] B = **(4.233, 4.900] GiB**.

4.75 GiB sits near the safe upper end. `test_ledger.cpp` pins both receipts and both ends of the
interval to the byte. That makes it the Ledger's absolute anchor: a Claim off by one byte moves an end,
so a buffer the engine changes forces the calibration to be re-derived.

Each 0.70 GiB taken off the reserve is one more Slot on every Sparse layer. The decode tool's
`vram GPU g: … free after open X GB` line measures what the reserve really leaves. If X stays well
above the 1 GiB floor through a long run, the reserve can come down.

## The drift check: why the counts can't silently disagree

The Ledger and the decoder are two independent derivations of the same bytes:

- `ledger.cpp` writes each buffer from the config's widths and the engine's shapes. The shapes are
  `engine::glm53::engine_shapes()`: the Exchange sizes, `kJobSlots`, `kGroupBatch`,
  `sizeof(GemvJob)` and `sizeof(JobUpload)`, plus a `static_assert` that a Round's GEMV columns are its
  tokens.
- `Decoder::open` adds up what it passed to `cudaMalloc`:
  - `Allocations::bytes()` around each block it allocates (Exchange, the Sparse layers' tables, Round,
    Sweep);
  - `bytes()` accessors on `LayerScratch`, `DeviceAttention` (weights and caches separately),
    `DeviceDenseMlp` and `DeviceHead` (weights and scratch separately).

A buffer added to the engine but not to the Ledger therefore fails `open()`, naming the GPU, the Claim
and both byte counts. One residual case: a new `DeviceArray` member left out of its class's `bytes()`
*and* out of the Ledger is invisible to both. The `free after open` line catches it on hardware.

Every VRAM allocation the GLM engine makes is in a `create`/`upload` or an `Allocations` call during
`open()`. `cuda/glm53/grouped_moe`'s own `cudaMalloc` serves only the oracle and check tools. After the
Slot pool, `open()` takes only pinned host memory (the RAM tier).

## Tests

| check | result |
|---|---|
| `ds4n_tests -tc='Ledger*'`: 8 cases, 2,458 assertions | pass |
| full ctest (CPU, no GPUs attached) | 2/2 pass |
| mutation probe on `ledger.cpp` (`2026-11-22-ledger-mutants.{sh,out}`) | **12/12 killed** |

The properties tested:

- a position costs 95,232 B of Cache;
- with the Cohort equal to the capacity, the slope is the audit's 196,422.5 B a position;
- past the Cohort, only Cache and LayerScratch grow, by 95,232 B and 32 B a position (200 random
  trials);
- the Sweep grows with the Cohort alone;
- GPU 0 − GPU 1 = the head and the logits;
- Slots by hand, never more with more context;
- an asked count is kept, refused above the fit or outside [64, 256];
- the smaller card decides;
- the receipts and the interval ends.

Three mutants died only in the receipt test: the Expert scratch dropped, the Round's gate/up planes
halved, and the Full layers' indexer weights dropped. The difference tests alone left absolute Claims
unguarded.

A build gotcha found on the way: `cohort-build.sh` syncs with `tar`, which restores source mtimes. After
a mutation probe, ninja kept the last mutant's object until the source was touched. The probe script
now rebuilds after restoring.

## Interface changes

- `DecoderConfig::reserve_bytes` (3 GiB kept free from free memory) is gone. In its place is
  `runtime_reserve_bytes` (VRAM outside the Claims; default `kDefaultRuntimeReserve`). No caller set the
  old field.
- `slots_per_layer` is unchanged. 0 means as many as the Ledger fits; an asked count above the fit is
  now refused before the Backbone loads.
- New: `engine_shapes()`, `VramReport`, `Decoder::vram()`. `ds4n-glm53-decode` prints a `vram GPU g`
  line per GPU, and `ds4n-glm53-serve` prints one to stderr.
